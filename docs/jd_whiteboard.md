# Judge Dredd Core Rulebook — White Room Reference
*Extracted for use in the cyberpunk Redot game. Default setting year: 2131.*

---

## 1. SETTING TONE & WORLD

**Core premise:** A city of 400 million people where robots do 97% of all work, leaving 87% of citizens permanently unemployed, bored, and fractious. The Judges are the law — judge, jury, and sentencing officer on the spot. Democracy is crushed or tolerated at best. Life is cheap but not meaningless; the city has an absurdist, black-comic energy.

**The Mega-City One feel:**
- Everything is BIG. City blocks are 1.5km tall. Highways are dozens of lanes wide.
- "City Bottom" — the shadow world beneath the megways and blocks; buried streets from old cities, perpetual darkness, criminal underclass, mutants, refugees.
- Citizens have nothing to do. Boredom drives everything: crazes, Block Wars, cults, crime, insane fashions.
- The city is loud, bright, neon-blasted, and utterly relentless. Sirens never stop. Traffic never stops.
- Not grimdark — irreverent, absurd, satirical. Citizens name their blocks after 20th century celebrities and go to war over it.

**Population stratification:**
- Top floors (Double Letter: AA-AZ): wealthy "Double Letter" citizens. Luxy-Blocks at apex.
- Mid floors (A-Z): standard city block residents.
- City Bottom / sub-levels: criminal underclass, maintenance droids, forgotten communities of muties/trogs/perps.
- Conapts: mid-tier separate residential buildings for the slightly-better-off.
- Luxy-Blocks: ultra-luxury, servo-droids for every whim.
- Crock Blocks: retirement homes for OACs (Old Age Citizens, 75+), with euthanasiums.
- DP Camps: Displaced Person camps — prefab domes in waste ground for the homeless.

**Economy:** Citizens get Mega Dole (monthly city welfare payment). No work → still housed and fed. Crime or craze activity fills the rest of the time.

## 2. MEGA-CITY ONE GEOGRAPHY

**305 Sectors** — each a self-contained judicial area under a Sector House.

**Sector regions:**
- **MegCentral (1-20):** Pop 30M. Spaceports, Academy of Law.
- **MegEast (21-108):** Pop 100M. Grand Hall of Justice, Psi-Division HQ, Statue of Judgement, highest crime rates, smuggling through the docks.
- **MegSouth (109-160):** Pop 85M. Dust Belt industrial zone, Power Tower, food production.
- **MegWest (161-240):** Pop 75M. Borders Cursed Earth; mutant break-ins common. Apetown (genetically modified apes).
- **MegNorth (241-300):** Pop 95M. Corporate HQs, wealthy citizens.
- **North-West Hab Zone (301-305):** Cut off by Nuke Alley (radioactive strip). Sector 301 = "The Pit" — legendary high crime.

**Beyond the walls:**
- **Cursed Earth:** Radiation-blasted wasteland. Mutants, gangs, renegades. Penal work camps. Judges do "the Long Walk" into it when they retire.
- **Black Atlantic:** The ocean. Pollution makes it black. Smugglers work it.

**Key city infrastructure:**
- **Superslab:** The longest urban road on Earth. Dozens of lanes. Never stops.
- **Megways/Skedways:** The highway system weaving between blocks. Overzoom, Underzoom tubes.
- **Zoomtubes:** Pressurized transport tubes running between blocks and sectors.
- **Holding Posts:** Plastisteel posts on every street. Judges cuff arrested perps to them, call for Catch Wagons.
- **Iso-Cubes:** Prison cells. Sentences are measured in cube-time.
- **Resyk:** Body recycling plant. Corpses are recycled into 107 useful products each.

## 3. JUSTICE DEPARTMENT STRUCTURE

**The Judges** — trained from age 5, Academy of Law for 15 years. No personal relationships, no money, no families. Clones exist (e.g., Dredd himself is a clone of Chief Judge Fargo).

**Hierarchy:**
- Chief Judge → Council of Five → Senior Judges → Judges → Rookies
- Sector Chief commands each sector's Sector House

**Divisions:**
- **Street Division:** Patrol Judges. The daily enforcers. Three 8-hour watches (A/B/C-Watch).
- **Psi Division:** Psychic Judges. Pre-Cogs (predict crime), Trans-D (monitor parallel dimensions), K-Unit (offensive psionics), Psyker-Unit (tracks criminal psions).
- **Tek Division:** Technology. Forensics, Vehicles, Weapons, Space Sciences. Carry scanalyzers.
- **Med Division:** Combat medics + hospital staff. The "meat wagons."
- **SJS (Special Judicial Service):** Internal affairs. Investigates corrupt Judges. Feared even by other Judges. Can be corrupt themselves.
- **Wally Squad:** Undercover Judges. Infiltrate criminal organizations, go deep cover as citizens/perps. Often derided but vital.
- **PSU (Public Surveillance Unit):** Runs all street cameras, Spy-in-the-Sky drones. Everything recorded.
- **Space Corps:** Off-world enforcement and colony protection.

**Sector House layout:** Control room + comms center (top) → briefing rooms/offices → holding cubes (sub-level) → armoury. Iso Block and Psycho Block nearby.

**Watch Detail table (random patrol assignments):**
- General Patrol, Block Patrol, Block War Aftermath, Crime Blitz, Crowd Control, H Wagon Duty, **Mall Detail**, Pat Wagon Duty
- *Mall Detail specifically noted:* "located in one of Mega-City One's vast, labyrinthine, shopping malls that are the size of a city block. Mall Detail involves dealing with shoplifters, taps, irate shoppers and unscrupulous shopkeepers keen to con honest citizens out of their hard-scrounged credits."

**Justice Department disciplines for rogue Judges:**
1. Penal Colony on Titan (minimum 20 years, biologically modified to survive the atmosphere)
2. Cursed Earth Penal Work Camps (minimum 10 years, rad-storms and mutant raids)
3. Discharge
4. Demotion / desk duty

## 10. MUTANTS, CRAZIES & CREATURES

**Mental conditions:**
- **Future Shock (Futsism):** City overwhelms the mind → violent rampage. +3 Str/Dex, Int reduced to 2. Cured by 1d6+2 months psychotherapy.
- **Block Mania:** Psychotic loyalty to own block, hate for all others. Can be drug-induced (as war weapon). Antidote exists.
- **Lemming Syndrome:** Mass psychosis, compulsive desire to leap from heights.
- **Rumour Zombies:** Mob hysteria from vague rumors; groups act on "someone told someone he did something."
- **Stookie Gland Addiction:** Addict needs monthly doses of anti-aging alien gland serum. Denial = rapid aging to death.
- **Petty Crime Addiction:** Compulsive shoplifting, vandalism, etc. Treated with Perp-Aid or iso-cube.
- **Spontaneous Confession Compulsion (Sponts):** Addicted to false confessions to Judges.

**Mutant trait examples (for flavor):**
- Absorbing: drains characteristics on contact
- Acid Exuder: acidic skin spray
- Droning: sedating buzz (End 8+ or be pacified for 1d6 min)
- Intangible: phases out of physical reality for 1d3 rounds
- Mimic: copies the physical form of organic creatures it touches
- Mournful: so sad observers can't bring themselves to harm it
- Scintillating: produces calming/hypnotic light display

**Creatures:**
- **Rad Rats:** Pack hunters in the undercity. Poisonous bite (1d6 damage/hour without vaccine). Attacked in numbers.
- **Sewer-Gators:** 5m blind albino alligators in the undercity sewers. Detect by hearing and smell. Decapitate prey in single bite.
- **Dog Vultures (Cursed Earth):** Flying mutant pack hunters. Scavengers who flee from force. Never attack outnumbering foes.
- **Kleggs:** 4m bipedal alien crocodile mercenaries. Payment: fresh meat only. Nearly impervious to small arms. Bite attack 2d6+1.
- **Klegghounds:** Reptilian tracking dogs. Track by taste. 100km pursuit range. Work with Kleggs.

## 12. QUEST HOOKS FOR OUR GAME

**Direct structural parallels:**

**Hub as city block / mall complex:**
- The MallHub is equivalent to a Mega-City One city block's lower levels: shops, services, different factions controlling different sections, a perpetual hum of bored citizens looking for stimulation.
- The mall has its own "block assembly" equivalent — whoever controls the information controls the politics.
- Mall Detail (Watch Detail) is literally described as dealing with "shoplifters, taps, irate shoppers and unscrupulous shopkeepers."

**Faction / area control model (Mersmeyer Tower analog):**
- Block analogy: each major gang/faction controls a "zone" of the hub. Neutral zones maintained by leverage (like Sector Chief DiMaggio and Contralto).
- A "Dr. Meppen" equivalent: a neutral service provider (med bay, info broker, fence) who uses necessity as protection.
- Faction war threshold: like Block War — brewing rivalries, specific trigger events, an agitator who wants war started.

**Quest hook seeds (adapted from Sector 13):**

1. **The Disappearances** — Citizens from a specific wing/zone going missing. No bodies. No witnesses. The obvious suspect (organlegger) checks out clean. Who's taking them?

2. **The Spaceport / Transit Hub Drug Run** — Someone is smoothing the way for off-world chemicals coming through the hub's transit links. Someone on the inside is greased. Classic "follow the money" investigation with Contralto-style misdirection.

3. **The Festival / Event Violence** — A regular hub event (market day? fight night?) is predicted to explode. A 74.6% chance of gang violence. The faction chiefs deny it. The player has to prevent it or manage it.

4. **Mob War Premonition** — Someone with precognitive ability (psyker NPC?) sees a coming war "under a full moon" or equivalent. The authority figure dismisses it. Player must either confirm or defuse.

5. **The Agitator** — A "Block Agitator" type NPC is running a secret propaganda campaign through the hub's info network, inflaming faction tensions. Find them before the war starts.

6. **The Snitch Network** — A Lloyd H Conover Block analog: one hub faction is deeply embedded with security/surveillance and trading tip-offs for protection. Another faction wants to destroy them. Player caught in the middle.

7. **The Missing Bodies (Resyk angle)** — The hub has its own equivalent of Resyk. Someone is using it illegally — either to dispose of evidence or to harvest organs/materials. A "meat wagon" connection.

8. **The Talent Sponsor** — Like Max Contralto sponsoring young Donny Osmosis, a crime boss wants to invest in a player-connected NPC (musician? fighter? scrawler?). Accepting the sponsorship creates faction entanglements.

9. **The Numbers Racket** — Someone is selling access codes to the hub's security systems. Once those codes are out, any gang can bypass locks, cameras, and alarms. Find the source before they're sold.

10. **The Face-Change Clinic** — An illegal Nu-You equivalent operating in the sub-levels. Wanted perps are using it to change identities. But the operator keeps records. Those records are suddenly worth killing for.

## 13. TONE CALIBRATION NOTES

**Judge Dredd's tone vs IZ2.0:**
- JD is more *absurdist* and *satirical* where IZ2 is more *gritty/transhumanist*. JD has blocks going to war because one named itself after Bon Jovi and the other after Springsteen.
- Both share: lived-in, cynical about institutions but warm about individual moments. Cities that feel real because they're ridiculous in a recognizable way.
- JD adds: bureaucratic absurdity as comedy. The Justice Department is both terrifying and ridiculous — sentencing someone to 5 years for littering is both a punchline and a genuine threat.
- JD adds: **scale of boredom** as a world-building principle. The game asks: what do 400 million people with nothing to do invent? Answer: block wars, illegal candy, spray-on body hair, illegal Boing®, fake grass sports, robot-punching booths.

**NPC dialogue cues (blend IZ2 and JD):**
- Citizens don't mention crime directly — they talk around it with slang.
- "He got chump dumped" = informer who outlived his usefulness, put in a position where he'll be destroyed.
- "Going the Long Walk" = someone who's given up / going somewhere they won't return from.
- "Fifty years in the cubes" = clearly exaggerated threat but a real sentence structure exists.
- "Your block or mine?" = Block War-coded greeting between rivals.
- "Drokker" = general insult (stronger version of "drokk").
- "I'm clean, Judge!" = reflexive protest even when doing nothing wrong.

**The mall specifically:**
- Every city block has its own mall at lower levels. The mega-mall (city block-sized) never closes. Sells everything. Controlled by someone.
- "Who runs the mart?" — The book literally asks this as the first question in mall design.
- Mall criminal involvement: "Are the criminal gangs involved in any way? What peculiar things can be bought in the malls that cannot be found in any other sectors?"
- Fire risk and structural danger are legitimate mall threats (the book mentions both).
