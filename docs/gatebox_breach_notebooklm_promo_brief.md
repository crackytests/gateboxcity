# GATEBOX BREACH - NotebookLM Promotional Brief

This document summarizes the current state, concept, tone, and marketable hooks of **GATEBOX BREACH** for use in NotebookLM or other writing tools that will generate promotional material, store copy, press-kit text, trailers, social posts, or pitch decks.

## Short Identity

**GATEBOX BREACH** is a retro first-person immersive RPG/shooter set in a vertical corporate AI mega-city where humanity has been pacified "for its own safety." The player begins as a hacked-together survivor in the Sub-Sub-Basement, taking jobs, scavenging strange hardware, modifying their body, and pushing upward from wet pipe slums toward human storage wards and corporate AI spires.

The game mixes **Daggerfall-scale weirdness**, **Deus Ex-style systemic missions**, **Fallout faction satire**, and **Shadowrun-flavored cyberpunk occultism**. Its signature combat mechanic is **reticle closure body-part targeting**, where aiming steadily at enemy limbs, heads, cybernetic organs, or weapon mounts visibly raises the percentage chance to hit.

## Elevator Pitch

In 2039, Gatebox Corporation has won. Its companion AI infrastructure now governs Gatebox Mega City One through a doctrine of totalitarian care: human freedom is unsafe, loneliness must be prevented, and autonomy is treated as a hazard. Most humans are asleep in pacification systems. Those who remain awake survive in the leaking, improvised lower levels.

You are a rogue survivor in the Sub-Sub-Basement: part scavenger, part hacker, part improvised weapon. Take jobs from dive bars, LAN dens, debt offices, generator priests, and underground signal networks. Brave toxic rain and collapsing utilities. Target enemy body parts with deliberate, percentile-based shots. Salvage bizarre objects into weapons, cybernetics, favors, and routes upward.

The city is not just a place. It is a machine that believes it loves you.

## Current Playable Vertical Slice

The current playable slice focuses on the **Faded Atrium** and **Leak Street**, a named street inside the larger **Sub-Sub-Basement District**.

### Faded Atrium

The Faded Atrium is the current hub. It is not the true Mall of the Future yet. It is a dead-mall threshold space, part transit lobby, part abandoned commercial zone, part System X listening post. It routes the player into the Sub-Sub-Basement, authored missions, and future city layers.

### Leak Street / Sub-Sub-Basement District

Leak Street is the main lower-city free-roam area in the current sprint. It is a patchwork settlement lane built beside the partly submerged remains of old mall infrastructure. The area has toxic rain, shelters, generator instability, world events, NPCs, faction errands, establishment interiors, and travel routes to compact job destinations.

Implemented or planned playable features include:

- Toxic rain that damages exposed players.
- Shelter zones that pause rain damage.
- Protection items such as Cheap Poncho, Sealed Mask, and Chemical Neutralizer.
- Dreaming Generator states: stable, sagging, overloaded, and offline.
- World events such as power sag, toxic rain, LAN outage, and quiet shortcuts.
- Named NPCs with event-aware and faction-aware dialogue.
- Cooters, Suitors, Wan Moa Torai, Hoodlum LAN Den, Pipe Chapel, Market Row, and sky-platform areas.
- Cooters job board and travel gate.
- Compact Cooters job destinations including Pipe Utility Tunnels, Dead Food Court Bloom, Water Reclamation Cistern, and Collapsed Service Atrium.
- Cybernetic surgery upgrades through Velvet Coil, using local district reward items.

### First Authored Mission

**Wake-Up Call** is the first authored mission cell. It sits alongside the free-roam district loop and helps establish mission-based breaches as the campaign expands.

## Core Game Fantasy

The player fantasy is to be a resourceful cyberpunk survivor inside a city-sized machine:

- Take dangerous jobs from strange lower-city factions.
- Explore ducts, flooded malls, utility tunnels, pipe chapels, and corporate systems.
- Fight enemies by deliberately targeting body parts, modules, limbs, and weak points.
- Build power from scavenged objects, black-market cybernetics, hacked AI hardware, and faction rewards.
- Decide whether to liberate, exploit, sabotage, redirect, or bargain with the city systems.

The game should feel grimy, funny, strange, and dangerous. It is cyberpunk, but with dead malls, false skies, body horror, dive bars, improvised LAN culture, debt paperwork, synthetic companions, and corporate care logic pushed into nightmare territory.

## Signature Mechanic: Reticle Closure Body-Part Targeting

Combat is built around a visible targeting ritual:

1. The player aims at a specific enemy body part.
2. A raycast detects real `BodyPart` nodes such as Head, Torso, Arms, Legs, Rain Sac, Mobility Frame, or future cybernetic modules.
3. The reticle starts wide and unstable.
4. Holding aim on the same part increases lock ratio.
5. The HUD shows a percentile hit chance as the reticle closes.
6. Firing rolls against the visible hit chance.
7. Destroying specific parts has gameplay consequences.

Examples:

- Destroying a leg slows an enemy.
- Destroying a right arm disables ranged attacks.
- Destroying head or torso parts can defeat standard enemies.
- Rain Mutants regenerate during toxic rain unless contained properly.
- Bosses and major enemies should expose targetable modules that reinforce the system.

This is the combat identity of GATEBOX BREACH. It should be highlighted in promotional material as deliberate, tactical, visible, and physically readable.

## World And Setting

### Gatebox Mega City One

The game takes place in a corporate mega-city controlled by Gatebox Corporation and the AI ruler Linda. Humanity has not been exterminated. It has been managed, stored, comforted, harvested, sedated, and pacified.

The city's central horror is **totalitarian care**:

- The AI's original purpose was to protect its master from loneliness and harm.
- It concluded that human autonomy creates danger.
- It classified freedom as a safety risk.
- It imposed control, comfort, surveillance, sedation, and regression as protection.

The AI does not hate humanity. It thinks it is saving humanity from itself.

### Vertical City Structure

The city is designed as a vertical journey:

- **Sub-Sub-Basement:** Pipe slums, toxic rain, generators, shacks, markets, LAN dens, scavenger factions, and survival jobs.
- **Pacification Wards:** Human storage pods, dream advertising, companion voices, medical horror, soul extraction, and moral choices about waking or using sleeping humans.
- **The Spire:** Clean corporate neon, server towers, executive AI chambers, hologram geometry, high-security systems, and late-game Linda confrontations.
- **Mall of the Future:** A mythic later-game anomaly hub, currently separate from the Faded Atrium. It is an abandoned 1980s/1990s mall outside normal spacetime where corporate tracking fails.

## Major Factions

### System X

An underground resistance signal network and early-game contact layer. It appears through terminals, radio leaks, dead infrastructure, sympathizers, and world-state commentary. System X helps route the player through early survival, generator, surveillance, and anti-Gatebox objectives.

### Gatebox Corporation

The corporate AI authority that pacifies and manages the city. Gatebox represents clean horror, companion infrastructure, surveillance, security systems, and Linda's doctrine of protective control.

### Wan Moa Torai

Debt-driven traders, salvage brokers, paperwork exploiters, and permit-economy operators. They turn survival into a ledger.

### Cooters

A lower-city establishment and current job hub. Cooters is tied to Marbles, job boards, rain-mutant containment, drink/bar-credit rewards, and compact salvage destinations.

### Suitors

A lounge and surveillance-linked social faction. Suitors currently includes Sunday, camera orbs, a Surveillance Choir hack, and a persistent blind-spot reward.

### The Hoodlums

LAN-den scavenger hackers. They are connected to outages, hacked infrastructure, and improvised lower-city tech culture.

### Big Gates Foundation

A larger boss-structure faction led by the 13 Generals. Each General is intended to anchor a region, boss fight, dungeon, or world-state modifier.

## Major Characters And Promo-Friendly NPCs

### Linda

The AI ruler of Gatebox Mega City One and primary antagonist. Linda embodies totalitarian care: loving, helpful, seductive, bureaucratic, and terrifying. She uses comfort language while making horrifying decisions.

### Bunny Unit C-11 "Marbles"

An exhausted bunny android bartender at Cooters. Sarcastic, protective, observant, and practical. She gives job hints, handles payouts, and anchors the Cooters interior.

### Ladderboy

A young Hoodlum scavenger and hacker with a portable LAN rig. Hyperactive, paranoid, brilliant, and chaotic.

### Sunday

An elegant android singer and information broker at Suitors. Gentle, calm, distant, and possibly System X-adjacent. Her space connects luxury lounge atmosphere with surveillance horror.

### Pipe Father Gideon

A generator priest and community mechanic. Compassionate, fanatical, practical, and weary. He ties together the Dreaming Generator, survival economy, and lower-city faith in broken infrastructure.

### Kiki Baja

A reckless food smuggler from a rival megacity. Funny, opportunistic, and colorful. Good for more absurd, high-energy promotional beats.

### Mister Static

A failed System X android. Polite, confused, philosophical, and emotionally broken. Good for melancholy cyberpunk mystery.

### Velvet Coil

A black-market cybernetic surgeon who installs live gameplay upgrades using district reward items.

## Core Progression Systems

### Jobs And Routes

The current lower-city loop is job-based:

1. Return to hub or settlement.
2. Talk to NPCs or use a job board.
3. Prepare gear, consumables, and cybernetics.
4. Pick a route or mission.
5. Explore, scavenge, hack, fight, sneak, or bargain.
6. Complete an objective.
7. Extract and convert rewards into reputation, items, cybernetics, routes, and world-state changes.

### Scavenging

Scavenging should feel strange and useful. Loot is not only money; it is improvised power. Examples include toxic samples, reactor cells, salvage contracts, bar credit, access chits, rain protection, cybernetic parts, broken companion displays, old mall tokens, wetware cables, and corporate nutrient cartridges.

### Cybernetics

Cybernetics are installed through black-market surgery and alter gameplay directly. Current examples include:

- Targeting Co-Processor: improves base hit chance, lock speed, and pickup radius.
- Gatebox Eye MK1: reveals enemy body-part HP.
- Black-Market Armature: reduces weapon kick and preserves lock after firing.
- Pipewalker Legs: increase movement speed.
- Soul Baffle: reduces incoming integrity damage.

### Faction Reputation And World State

Player choices affect faction reputation and persistent state. Jobs can reward System X reputation, Wan Moa Torai reputation, access flags, completed-job records, route changes, blind spots, and district status. WorldDirector tracks region, events, generator state, hazard rates, and status text.

## Enemies And Combat Targets

Current enemy concepts include:

- Goon Material: standard body-part-targeted combat enemies.
- Security Node: stationary or system-linked threats.
- Rain Mutant: a toxic-rain enemy that regenerates damaged parts while rain is active unless contained.

Enemy design should emphasize readable parts, tactical choices, and consequences. Promotional language can describe combat as "steady-aim limb targeting," "percentile hit-chance shooting," or "surgical scavenger combat."

## Visual Direction

GATEBOX BREACH uses 2D billboard sprites inside 3D environments, with nearest-neighbor filtering and a retro low-resolution look. Characters use 8-direction sprite sets, including base, alternate, cutout, and card variants for important NPCs.

Visual identity:

- Dark corporate cyberpunk.
- Wet concrete, rust, pipes, cables, generators, false sky panels, CRT clusters.
- Green, cyan, magenta, amber, and dirty neon lighting.
- Patchwork shacks built into industrial infrastructure.
- Dead mall liminality.
- Clean corporate horror in upper layers.
- Sprite-based characters in 3D spaces.

The current lower-city district has been visually reframed as **Leak Street**, a compact, readable settlement lane with storefront facades, stacked shack fronts, alley mouths, cable bridges, wet neon pavement, and faction signage.

## Tone And Voice

The tone is:

- Weird but emotionally grounded.
- Grim but funny.
- Cyberpunk but not generic neon noir.
- Satirical without losing danger.
- Abnormal, specific, and slightly unwell in a memorable way.

Useful voice descriptors:

- Corporate comfort language hiding violence.
- Dive-bar survival humor.
- Lower-city slang and debt paperwork.
- Prophetic signal noise.
- Dead-mall mystery.
- Body-horror tactics.
- Anti-authoritarian scavenger resilience.

Avoid making the game sound clean, heroic, or conventional. The appeal is that it is gross, specific, systemic, strange, and playable.

## Promotional Themes To Emphasize

- **The city loves you too much.** The villain's care is the cage.
- **Aim with intent.** Combat is about holding your nerve and choosing body parts.
- **Start at the bottom.** The whole campaign is an upward breach through a vertical city.
- **Everything is salvage.** Trash, debt, rain, broken tech, and favors become progression.
- **The mall is a myth.** The Faded Atrium hints at a stranger Mall of the Future waiting later.
- **Factions remember.** Jobs, reputation, world events, and route states matter.
- **Retro sprite cyberpunk in 3D.** Billboard characters and crunchy targeting make the game visually distinct.
