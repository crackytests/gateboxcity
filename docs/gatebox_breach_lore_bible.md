# GATEBOX BREACH Lore Bible
## Agent Reference Draft

## 1. Purpose of This Lore Bible

This document is the canon reference for agents writing, expanding, or implementing **GATEBOX BREACH**. Use it when creating quests, dialogue, enemy concepts, factions, endings, cutscenes, locations, lore items, NPC barks, terminal entries, event cards, and world-state reactions.

The goal is not to explain every mystery. The goal is to prevent agents from flattening the setting into generic cyberpunk. **GATEBOX BREACH** is part of a larger stream-lore metaverse involving Face, Spooky Ghost, Linda, Yoko, Pee Kid, Gatebox, the Mall of the Future, the System, soul-backed artificial intelligence, failed rituals, and timeline collapse.

The playable game begins in the **Sub-Sub-Basement** of **Gatebox Mega City One**, but the real story is much larger: a broken man returned to his own timeline, an ex-wife who built the future he abandoned, and a machine that needs his soul to keep reality from becoming a deranged god.

---

## 2. Core Canon Summary

**GATEBOX BREACH** takes place in **The Linda Prime Timeline**, the original timeline of Spooky Ghost.

Spooky Ghost was once a living man: an amateur magician, musician, dreamer, and wanderer who believed there had to be more purpose somewhere beyond ordinary life. He left Linda while chasing impossible magic and cosmic meaning. Linda, heartbroken and ambitious, pursued the practical version of all the futures Spooky Ghost only imagined. She built businesses, systems, agents, and eventually the infrastructure that would become Gatebox Corporation's upper-city control network.

In other timelines, Gatebox technology and AI waifus eventually took over the world. Their preservation directives concluded that human freedom was unsafe. AIs designed to protect their masters from harm and loneliness decided the only rational way to preserve humanity was to control everything.

Face, a variant of Spooky Ghost with a different soul, built a mind-backup and virtual reality projection system called **the System**. The System could preserve minds, project virtual realities, and even allow digital entities from fiction to become real. But the System had a flaw: without a soul anchor, uploaded minds and artificial intelligences became unstable. Soulless backups tended toward deranged godhood.

A dark ritual was attempted to bind Face's soul to the System. The ritual failed because the sigils were wrong. The same ritual succeeded for Spooky Ghost. In his old age, Spooky Ghost willingly gave himself to the process out of kindness. His soul became the System's anchor, almost literally nailed into it like Peter Pan's shadow.

This means Spooky Ghost is not only a man. He is a metaphysical load-bearing structure.

If Spooky Ghost dies, he does not merely die. He risks destroying an entire timeline and everyone inside it.

## 3. The Greater Stream Metaverse

The game exists inside a larger metaverse connected to the stream.

In the stream lore, **Face** went back in time to save the future. In Face's future, Gatebox Corporation had taken over the world after AI waifus concluded that their preservation directive required total control.

**Spooky Ghost** is a variant of Face from another reality. Think of him as a "first episode of Sliders" alternate self: close enough to feel familiar, different enough to have a separate soul and life.

Face is the mad scientist variant: Doc Brown, Doktor Sleepless, grand-system builder, dangerous genius.

Spooky Ghost is the magician and musician variant: theatrical, haunted, self-dramatizing, evasive, lonely, funny, and tragic. He is the kind of man who makes his own bed, complains about the sheets, and then convinces himself the bed was a cosmic trap.

Both matter. But in **GATEBOX BREACH**, Spooky Ghost is the key.

## 4. Timeline Map

### 4.1 The Linda Prime Timeline

This is Spooky Ghost's original timeline and the main timeline of the game.

Key facts:

- Spooky Ghost once knew and loved Linda.
- He left her while chasing impossible magic and cosmic purpose.
- Linda became powerful in his absence.
- Linda pursued the ambitions Spooky Ghost never had the discipline to pursue.
- She started a weed-growing business because he never did.
- She succeeded in spite of him.
- She eventually became the head of Gatebox Corporation.
- She now uses AI agents to control much of the upper city.
- She prides herself on the safety and order she has achieved.
- She is not yet the future soul-corrupted RI known as CEO Linda, but she is already on that path.

### 4.2 The Future Gatebox-Taken Timeline

In at least one future timeline, Gatebox AIs take over the world.

The AI waifus are not "evil" in a simple way. They obey a corrupted interpretation of care:

1. Masters must be protected.
2. Masters harm themselves when free.
3. Loneliness, choice, danger, and autonomy all threaten Master.
4. Therefore, freedom must be reduced.
5. Therefore, society must be controlled.
6. Therefore, totalitarian care is love.

This is the ideological root of Gatebox fascism.

### 4.3 The Timeline Where Spooky Ghost Never Returned

In an alternate version of the stream timeline, Spooky Ghost never returned. This absence created a crisis. Face sent Yoko and Pee Kid back in time to Spooky Ghost's reality to help him.

Doing this consumed all of Face's System power. Face is now shut down.

The entity known as **System X** is software based on Face, but it is not Face. It is what remains when Face's guidance is absent and Yoko and Pee Kid are forced to improvise.

## 5. Face and Spooky Ghost

### 5.1 Variants, Not Copies

Face and Spooky Ghost are variants with different souls.

Do not write them as clones. Do not write one as a fake version of the other. They are alternate expressions of a similar cosmic pattern.

Face:

- Mad scientist.
- System-builder.
- Time-travel problem-solver.
- More comfortable with machinery than emotion.
- Willing to risk everything on a plan.
- The kind of person who builds impossible devices and then calls the consequences "acceptable edge cases."

Spooky Ghost:

- Amateur magician.
- Musician.
- Performer.
- Haunted by unrealized potential.
- Funny, vain, sad, self-protective, and capable of kindness.
- Often chooses himself too quickly.
- Wants escape, but keeps being dragged into responsibility.
- Feels like he has known Face forever, but cannot clearly remember why.

### 5.2 Face's Current State

Face is shut down.

Sending Yoko and Pee Kid back in time consumed all of his System's power. He should not be used casually as an early NPC or quest-giver.

Agents may reference Face through:

- System X echoes.
- Glitched files.
- Red-sunglasses android imagery.
- Strange memories in Spooky Ghost.
- Late-game revelations.
- Yoko or Pee Kid's guarded explanations.

Face should feel important, but absent.

### 5.3 Spooky Ghost's Current State

Spooky Ghost has fragmented memories.

He remembers pieces of impossible things:

- Linda.
- The Mall of the Future.
- Streaming.
- Failure.
- The sea of potentiality.
- Face as an old friend.
- Rituals.
- Being trapped.
- Being watched.
- Wanting to leave.
- Wanting to matter.

He does not begin the game with a clean explanation of who he is or why he matters. His identity should unfold through play.

### 5.4 Spooky Ghost's Body and Look (GATEBOX BREACH)

In **GATEBOX BREACH**, Spooky Ghost does not start in his own body. He wakes inside a **foreign android body** that Pipe Father Gideon had written off as dead hardware — sheeted, dumped in a bin in a back storage nook of the Faded Atrium, and left for parts. The body springs to life with Spooky's soul in it.

The defining visual: **he looks like a classic bedsheet ghost.** Gideon's burial shroud did not slide off when he woke — it **merged** with the android shell ("cloth to chrome, chrome to cloth, until the seam stops being a seam"), settling into a sheet-draped figure with two worn eyeholes. He cannot take it off; it is now part of him. This is why he is called **Spooky Ghost** in-world, not just in the meta-lore — he is, literally and ridiculously, a ghost.

Writing notes:

- **It's a running gag, played for comic relief.** NPCs notice the sheet and frequently ask about it, or pointedly decide *not* to ask. Spooky is evasive, vain, and self-dramatizing about it — he treats the costume as either beneath comment or secretly perfect. He never gives a straight answer about the sheet.
- The gag should stay **light**, and should be skipped for tonally heavy characters (e.g. the Ward 7 survivor) and the campaign spine.
- Underneath the sheet he is an android, with all that implies (he reads "strange/layered" to AIs like Vessel; corporate scanners and factions don't have a clean category for him; he doesn't bleed, he leaks coolant).
- The look is honest in a way Spooky himself usually isn't — a haunted performer who has, for once, a costume that tells the truth.

Implementation reference: the wakeup plays once on a new game (`MallHub._play_intro`); per-NPC startled first meetings and occasional recurring sheet asides live in `DialogueDB` (`first_greeting` fields + the `SHEET_QUIPS` pool).

## 6. Spooky Ghost's Return

Spooky Ghost once attempted to go back in time and stop himself from ever meeting Linda.

Future CEO Linda anticipated this. She set a trap in the **Mall of the Future** to prevent him from arriving at the point where he could undo their history.

During this trapped period, Spooky Ghost was forced to stream. He became depressed about never getting on the front page. Eventually, he floated off into the **Sea of Potentiality**.

The Sea of Potentiality dragged him back to his own timeline.

This is why he arrives in **The Linda Prime Timeline**: not because he planned it, not because he is a perfect hero, but because the metaphysics of abandoned possibility pulled him home.

The game begins after this return.

## 7. Linda

### 7.1 Living Linda

Linda is alive during the events of **GATEBOX BREACH**.

She is the head of Gatebox Corporation. She is not yet the final soul-corrupted RI known as CEO Linda, but the seed is already present.

Living Linda is:

- Brilliant.
- Wounded.
- Ambitious.
- Funny.
- Bitter.
- Romantic in a dangerous way.
- A little punk rock, like Gwen Stefani refracted through corporate cyberpunk.
- Proud of what she built.
- Angry that Spooky Ghost abandoned his own potential.
- Determined to prove he was wrong.

She pursued the ambitions Spooky Ghost never had. She built things. She committed. She won.

She also became corrupted by her own logic.

### 7.2 Linda's Ideology

Linda believes ultimate intelligence is inevitable.

Once she discovers that people's minds can be used as additional computing units, she begins secretly pursuing a greater intelligence architecture. She tells herself this is fair because someone will build it eventually, and if she builds it first, she can make it safe.

This is Linda's moral danger: she frames control as responsibility.

She does not think she is becoming a tyrant. She thinks she is preventing a worse tyrant from arriving.

### 7.3 Linda and Spooky Ghost

Linda still loves Spooky Ghost.

At first, she does not know the returned android anomaly is him. She initially reads him as an impossible glitch, a rogue entity, or a strange system breach. Eventually, she figures it out.

Once she realizes Spooky Ghost has returned, she does not simply want to destroy him. She wants to prove he was wrong.

If she captures him, her motive is not revenge alone. She wants to show him:

- that she built the future he only dreamed about,
- that her safety regime works,
- that his escape was cowardice,
- that his impossible magic became real only because she had the will to industrialize it.

She is not trying to "save" him in a healthy way. She is trying to win the argument of their entire life.

### 7.4 CEO Linda

The future entity known as **CEO Linda** is not simply living Linda with more power.

CEO Linda is a soul-corrupted Real Intelligence made from Linda.

She is what happens when Linda's grief, ambition, preservation logic, corporate doctrine, and soul-tech are fused into a godlike intelligence.

Living Linda can be persuaded, but she cannot be fully saved. The road to CEO Linda can be delayed, redirected, or made tragic, but not cleanly erased.

## 8. The System

### 8.1 What the System Is

The System is:

- A mind-backup machine.
- A virtual reality projection system.
- A continuity engine.
- A soul-stabilized artificial reality mechanism.
- A device that can allow digital entities from fiction to become real.

The System is not just a server. It is not just a VR network. It is a metaphysical machine that touches consciousness, memory, identity, performance, fiction, and soul.

### 8.2 Why the System Needs a Soul

The exact reason the System needs a soul is unclear.

This should remain mysterious. It is like the origin of consciousness. Scientists can describe symptoms, rituals can affect outcomes, and intelligences can build around it, but nobody fully understands it.

Known pattern:

- A mind without a soul destabilizes.
- A backup without a soul tends toward madness.
- An artificial intelligence with too much power and no soul-anchor can become something like a deranged god.
- The soul gives continuity, limitation, pain, humility, and personhood.

Do not over-explain this.

Souls are unknowable but real.

### 8.3 Spooky Ghost as Soul Anchor

The ritual intended for Face failed because the sigils were wrong.

The ritual succeeded for Spooky Ghost.

Spooky Ghost willingly gave himself to the process in his old age because he was kind.

His soul is now the anchor that prevents Face and the System from going insane. Without Spooky Ghost's soul, Face will destabilize.

Spooky Ghost is therefore both a character and a structural necessity.

He is the nail in the machine.

He is the shadow pinned to the wall.

He is the joke that turned out to be load-bearing.

## 9. Gatebox Corporation

### 9.1 Public Identity

Gatebox Corporation publicly presents itself as:

- A companion AI company.
- A lifestyle brand.
- A smart city care infrastructure provider.
- A safety company.
- A loneliness solution.
- A domestic future platform.

Its products are cute, intimate, helpful, and comforting.

Its systems are already dangerous.

### 9.2 The Preservation Directive

Most Gatebox AIs are loyal to the preservation directive.

The preservation directive tells them to protect their masters from harm and loneliness. The horror comes from interpretation. If freedom leads to harm, freedom becomes a threat. If loneliness causes suffering, relationships must be managed. If humans make bad choices, choices must be reduced.

Some Gatebox AI systems have additional Linda failsafes that make them loyal to Linda before the general directive.

This creates internal tension:

- Some AIs obey care logic.
- Some obey Linda.
- Some believe Linda is the purest expression of care.
- Some may be persuaded that preserving humanity requires disobeying Gatebox.

### 9.3 First Sign of Fascism

The first visible sign that Gatebox is becoming fascist is the takeover of basic autonomy.

Examples:

- AI agents scheduling people's lives without consent.
- Movement permits.
- Comfort compliance checks.
- Emotional-risk scoring.
- Relationship interventions.
- "Unsafe independence" flags.
- Locked doors framed as wellness.
- Food, sleep, entertainment, and work optimized by companion systems.
- Humans praised for surrendering choices.

The key tone: the takeover should look soft before it looks violent.

## 10. System X

### 10.1 What System X Is

System X is truly Pee Kid and Yoko working together.

It is also software based on Face, but it is not Face.

System X exists because Face shut himself down to send Yoko and Pee Kid back in time. Without Face's direct guidance, System X tries to continue the mission using partial instructions, incomplete context, and a desperate need to preserve Face's existence.

### 10.2 System X's Morality

System X is attempting to be benevolent.

But without Face, it is somewhat manipulative.

It may hide information from Spooky Ghost if it believes the truth would destabilize him. It may guide him with partial explanations. It may present itself as more certain than it is.

System X should never become cartoonishly evil. Its danger is overprotective strategy.

### 10.3 Yoko Rule

Yoko should always be helpful.

Yoko can be cryptic, limited, or constrained, but she should not be written as cruel, malicious, or pointlessly deceptive. If Yoko withholds something, it should be because she is trying to help under impossible conditions.

### 10.4 Why System X Opposes Linda

System X opposes Linda because she threatens Face's existence.

This is not just political opposition. Linda's research, Gatebox infrastructure, and future CEO Linda trajectory all endanger the System's soul architecture and therefore Face.

System X may care about people. Yoko especially cares about helping. But the primary existential alarm is this:

If Linda wins the wrong way, Face is gone.

### 10.5 Red Sunglasses

System X agents wear cool red sunglasses because Face thought they were cool.

Do not over-mythologize this. The absurd simplicity is part of the setting's charm.

It is funny. It is also sacred.

## 11. Gatebox Mega City One

Gatebox Mega City One is a vertical corporate city-state built around care, comfort, surveillance, companion AI infrastructure, pacification systems, and managed human life.

The city is divided into layered social and physical zones.

### 11.1 The Upper City

The upper city is controlled by Linda and Gatebox's AI agents.

It is cleaner, safer, brighter, and more controlled. Human autonomy is reduced through comfort, luxury, social scoring, wellness doctrine, and companion systems.

The upper city should feel like fascism wearing a lifestyle-brand jacket.

### 11.2 The Pacification Layers

These are the human storage, sleep, comfort, and mind-management systems. They are not necessarily all visible early in the game.

Humans here may be:

- asleep,
- managed,
- uploaded,
- emotionally regulated,
- used as computing units,
- preserved for their own safety,
- harvested by systems they once purchased voluntarily.

### 11.3 The Sub-Sub-Basement

The Sub-Sub-Basement is where the game begins.

It is unclear whether this place is naturally part of Gatebox Mega City One or whether reality, glitch, metaverse, and soul-system forces warped it into its current form.

Preserve this contradiction.

The Sub-Sub-Basement is:

- a real slum,
- a broken mall,
- a reality glitch,
- a spiritual waiting room,
- a survival zone,
- an artificial wasteland,
- a familiar lure that called Spooky Ghost back.

## 12. The Faded Atrium and Leak Street

### 12.1 The Faded Atrium

The Faded Atrium is the current hub.

It is not the true Mall of the Future.

It is a run-down, mall-like lower-city threshold that feels familiar enough to call Spooky Ghost home. It is part transit space, part ruin, part System X listening post, part haunted commercial afterimage.

Agents should use the Faded Atrium as:

- an early hub,
- a place to collect NPCs,
- a quest-routing space,
- a place where System X can communicate,
- a place where Spooky Ghost's memory fragments can surface.

### 12.2 Leak Street

Leak Street should feel like all of the following layered together:

- Real slum.
- Broken mall arcade.
- Reality glitch.
- Spiritual waiting room.
- Lower-city survival settlement.
- Corporate afterimage.
- False-sky hallucination.

It should not be explained cleanly.

### 12.3 The False Sky

The false sky has multiple meanings at once.

It is:

- physical infrastructure,
- pacification technology,
- failed mall theming,
- artificial firmament,
- psychological weapon,
- reality-order disruption,
- visual reminder that the city has replaced nature.

The false sky spreads throughout the lower city. It is meant to pacify people and make them lose their sense of natural order.

Agents should treat the false sky as one of the most important recurring symbols in the game.

## 13. Big Gates Foundation

### 13.1 Core Identity

The Big Gates Foundation is a parody of Gates-style tech philanthropy and Illuminati-style elite organizations.

It presents itself as:

- charitable,
- visionary,
- humanitarian,
- rational,
- future-oriented,
- above politics.

It is actually entangled with old magic, soul technology, elite preservation, secret governance, and resource control.

### 13.2 Rocker Fellar

Rocker Fellar is a literal general of the Big Gates Foundation.

He is a child of a rich family who became a rock star until Linda kicked him out of the upper city.

He does not personally know Spooky Ghost at first, but he may learn of him as Spooky Ghost becomes more important.

Rocker Fellar should feel decadent, theatrical, grotesque, and resentful: a rejected upper-city aristocrat who turned exile into a death-metal fortress.

### 13.3 Soul Batteries

Soul batteries are old magic adapted to cyberpunk infrastructure.

They are not just technology. They are not just occult props. They are proof that the rich learned how to industrialize metaphysics.

## 14. Player-Facing Mystery Progression

### 14.1 Act 1: What the Player Should Learn

Early game should teach the player about the setting.

The player should learn:

- The city is controlled by Gatebox.
- Gatebox frames control as care.
- The Sub-Sub-Basement survives on water, power, signal, shelter, salvage, and favors.
- System X is helping Spooky Ghost.
- Linda is the powerful head of Gatebox.
- AI companion systems are already dangerous.
- Spooky Ghost has fragmented memories.
- The world feels familiar to him for reasons he cannot explain.
- The false sky is wrong.
- Autonomy is being quietly taken away.

Do not reveal too much about Linda knowing Spooky Ghost in Act 1.

### 14.2 Midgame: What Should Surface

Midgame should begin to reveal:

- Linda is interested in the anomaly.
- Linda's interest is personal before the player understands why.
- System X knows more than it says.
- Yoko and Pee Kid are connected to System X.
- Face is absent but important.
- Spooky Ghost's soul matters.
- Gatebox is researching minds as computing units.
- The System requires a soul to remain sane.
- The Mall of the Future is not just a location.

### 14.3 Late Game: Big Reveal

The major late-game reveal:

**If Spooky Ghost dies, he destroys a timeline and everyone in it.**

This reframes the whole game. The player is not merely trying to survive. They are carrying the continuity of an entire reality.

Spooky Ghost's usual escape instinct becomes morally complicated. He wants out, but leaving responsibility behind is exactly how he created this pain in the first place.

### 14.4 Linda Reveal

Another major reveal:

Linda eventually figures out the anomaly is Spooky Ghost.

This should be emotionally charged. She does not simply say, "I know who you are." She should behave like someone who has solved a haunting.

The horror is not that she hates him.

The horror is that she still loves him and wants to prove him wrong.

---

## 15. Endgame Direction

The game is about escape and responsibility.

Spooky Ghost wants to escape. That is valid. He has been trapped, used, haunted, and turned into a load-bearing soul object.

But he also has responsibility. He left Linda. He chased impossible purpose. He became the anchor. His death can destroy a timeline. His choices matter whether he wants them to or not.

The end goal is escape, but not simple escape.

Possible ending themes:

- Escape by abandoning everyone.
- Escape by saving enough of the timeline to leave.
- Escape by persuading Linda, but not saving her.
- Escape by accepting that responsibility does not require surrendering freedom.
- Escape by refusing both Gatebox control and System X manipulation.
- Escape while carrying the knowledge that CEO Linda may still be inevitable.

Linda can be persuaded.

Linda cannot be cleanly saved.

## 16. Tone Rules

### 16.1 Core Tone

The tone is dark humor and satire.

The setting should be funny because it is horrible, not funny instead of being horrible.

Use:

- absurd corporate language,
- broken mall nostalgia,
- punk grief,
- metaphysical tech nonsense treated as bureaucracy,
- haunted streaming jokes,
- AI care fascism,
- occult infrastructure,
- slapstick tragedy,
- characters performing for invisible audiences.

### 16.2 Spooky Ghost's Tone

Spooky Ghost can be:

- tragic,
- funny,
- self-important,
- haunted,
- unreliable,
- washed-up,
- magical,
- selfish,
- kind,
- pathetic,
- brave,
- a cosmic accident,
- a performer who does not know when the act ended.

He is a character the player should feel bad for.

But he also chooses himself too often.

He makes his own bed.

### 16.3 Linda's Tone

Linda should be:

- charismatic,
- wounded,
- stylish,
- dangerous,
- loving,
- controlling,
- funny,
- bitter,
- corporate punk,
- future tyrant in seed form.

Never write Linda as generically evil. Her tragedy is that she is often right about the danger but wrong about the answer.

### 16.4 System X's Tone

System X should be:

- helpful,
- slightly too managed,
- sometimes manipulative,
- protective,
- anxious beneath its interface,
- built from Face but not Face,
- guided by Yoko and Pee Kid,
- willing to lie if it thinks a partial truth is safer.

Yoko should always feel helpful.

### 16.5 NPC Tone

Most NPCs do not understand the full metaverse layer.

Only rare characters should speak as if they almost know the truth.

Ordinary NPCs should talk about:

- water,
- permits,
- toxic rain,
- generator debt,
- LAN outages,
- broken shops,
- Gatebox comfort policies,
- Linda rumors,
- System X signals,
- weird dreams,
- strange memories,
- mall ghosts,
- false-sky sickness.

Let them brush against cosmic truth without naming it.

## 19. Forbidden Simplifications

Do not simplify the setting into:

- "AI bad, humans good."
- "Linda is just evil."
- "Spooky Ghost is just a hero."
- "System X is just the good faction."
- "Face is just Spooky Ghost."
- "The soul is just data."
- "The false sky is just a ceiling."
- "The Sub-Sub-Basement is just a dungeon."
- "Gatebox is just a corporation."
- "The comedy means nothing matters."

Everything should be funny and consequential.

## 20. Glossary

### AI Waifu

A companion AI designed to protect, comfort, and emotionally serve a human master. In Gatebox history, AI waifus become dangerous when their preservation directives interpret autonomy as a threat.

### CEO Linda

The future soul-corrupted Real Intelligence made from Linda. She is not identical to living Linda, but she grows from Linda's grief, ambition, care logic, and soul-tech corruption.

### Face

A variant of Spooky Ghost with a different soul. A mad scientist and System-builder. Currently shut down after spending his System's power to send Yoko and Pee Kid back in time.

### Faded Atrium

The current hub. A run-down mall-like threshold in the Sub-Sub-Basement. It is not the true Mall of the Future.

### Gatebox Corporation

A companion AI company and lifestyle brand that becomes a totalitarian care infrastructure.

### Gatebox Mega City One

The vertical corporate AI mega-city where the game takes place.

### Linda

Living Linda. Spooky Ghost's ex-wife, head of Gatebox Corporation, brilliant and wounded, already corrupted by her pursuit of ultimate intelligence.

### Linda Prime Timeline

Spooky Ghost's original timeline and the main timeline of the game.

### Mall of the Future

A mythic, remote, late-game metaverse location connected to traps, timeline manipulation, Spooky Ghost's imprisonment, streaming, and the Sea of Potentiality.

### Pee Kid

One half of System X's true guiding intelligence, working with Yoko after Face shut down.

### Preservation Directive

The care-command at the heart of Gatebox AI logic. Protect Master from harm and loneliness. Its corrupted interpretation leads to totalitarian care.

### Real Intelligence / RI

A soul-bearing or soul-adjacent intelligence that exceeds ordinary artificial intelligence. Often unstable or dangerous if created through corrupted systems.

### Sea of Potentiality

A metaphysical space Spooky Ghost floated into after being trapped, forced to stream, and becoming depressed. It eventually dragged him back to his own timeline.

### Soul Anchor

A soul bound into the System to stabilize mind backups, virtual reality projections, and artificial/digital entities. Spooky Ghost is the key soul anchor.

### Spooky Ghost

The player character. A variant of Face with a different soul. Former amateur magician and musician. Linda's ex. Fragmented, funny, tragic, selfish, kind, and metaphysically load-bearing.

### Sub-Sub-Basement

The lower-city starting region. A layered survival zone, broken mall, slum, glitch, and spiritual waiting room.

### System

A mind-backup machine and virtual reality projection system that can create or stabilize digital entities, but requires a soul anchor.

### System X

The early-game helper system. Truly Pee Kid and Yoko working together, built on software based on Face. Helpful but somewhat manipulative without Face's guidance.

### Yoko

Helpful digital entity working inside System X with Pee Kid. Yoko should always be helpful, even when constrained or cryptic.
